\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=lake!6,colframe=lake,title=\textbf{The rule},fonttitle=\sffamily]
The buyer pays the works, the delivery and, abroad, the insurance when the quote includes it:
\[
  \text{total} \;=\; \text{works} \;+\; \text{delivery} \;+\; \text{insurance}.
\]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The price of a work already includes VAT when the seller is an \emph{osek murshe}; a sale
    abroad is a zero-rated export.
  \item In Israel, delivery is a flat price: pickup, Israel Post home delivery, or the courier's
    price for the box size. Abroad, it is DHL Express's
    \emph{published} price for the box or, when the parcel fits, Israel Post EMS's published price;
    the site computes both and the buyer chooses.
  \item Duties, import taxes and clearance fees at the destination are the buyer's, paid to DHL on
    delivery (Incoterm DAP). The site never collects them.
  \item Payment and currency-conversion fees are the seller's, paid out of the price. They are
    never added to the buyer's total.
\end{itemize}
The quote is locked when the buyer presses ``Continue to payment''.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Delivery in Israel}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pickup from the studio} costs nothing, and the buyer types no address.
  \item \textbf{Israel Post home delivery} (counter to door, next day in most localities) at
    Israel Post's published price \cite{postdomestic}, by the parcel's actual weight: up to 1\,kg
    \nis{67}, up to 10\,kg \nis{73}, up to 20\,kg \nis{86} (the inter-city prices; within a
    city it costs \nis{45}, \nis{56}, \nis{67}). It is offered when the parcel fits Israel Post's
    limits (Section~\ref{sec:post}); most of her works up to $80\times80$\,cm do.
  \item \textbf{The courier} is priced per box from a table, by size class. The class comes from
    the packed box, sides sorted longest first, and its chargeable weight
    $\max(\text{actual}, \text{L}\times\text{W}\times\text{H}/5000)$:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item S: longest side $\le 50$\,cm and chargeable weight $\le 5$\,kg;
      \item M: longest $\le 100$\,cm, second $\le 80$\,cm, chargeable $\le 20$\,kg;
      \item L: anything larger, up to 45\,kg chargeable;
      \item a quote: a crate, glass or acrylic glazing, 25\,kg or more actual weight, more than
        45\,kg chargeable, a ``quote only'' work, or a tube for a work that cannot be rolled.
    \end{itemize}
    The table's shekel prices (seed: S \nis{60}, M \nis{120}, L \nis{250}) are placeholders. The
    painter copies her courier's price list into the table and presses ``Mark the rates as
    calibrated today''; until then going live is blocked (\code{RATES_UNCALIBRATED}). Israeli
    couriers publish price lists but no standard pricing API.
  \item Shipments in Israel are not insured.
\end{itemize}

\section{Delivery abroad: the DHL Express estimate}\label{sec:abroad}
The site has no DHL account yet, so it cannot ask DHL for a live price. Instead it applies DHL's
published price list for exports from Israel, the \emph{DHL Express Service \& Rate Guide 2026:
Israel} \cite{dhlguide}, together with the surcharges DHL publishes on its Israeli pages
\cite{dhlsur,dhldemand,dhlnotice,dhlcustoms}. The product is DHL Express Worldwide
(non-documents), the one the site books. The code is \code{src/server/shipping/dhl-estimate.ts};
the tariff data is in \code{src/server/shipping/tariff/}.

\subsection{The box and its billing weight}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each work travels in its packed box. The default box is the painting plus 12\,cm on each
    dimension, 11\,cm deep for a stretched canvas; the painter can enter the real box per work.
  \item DHL measures each side in whole centimetres, rounded up (as on the label), and computes
    the \emph{volumetric weight} $\text{L}\times\text{W}\times\text{H}/5000$\,kg.
  \item A piece bills at the higher of its actual and volumetric weight, rounded up to 0.5\,kg.
  \item The whole order is one multi-piece shipment, as fulfilment books it: the billing weight is
    the sum of the pieces, in 0.5\,kg steps up to 30\,kg and whole kilograms above. One shipment
    of two works costs less than two shipments.
\end{enumerate}
Paintings are large and light, so the volume decides. A $100\times80$\,cm canvas weighs 6.3\,kg
packed, but its $112\times92\times11$\,cm box bills at 23\,kg.

\subsection{Surcharges}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Piece surcharges}, at most one per piece \cite{dhlsur}: \emph{Overweight}
    \nis{395} above 70\,kg (actual or volumetric); otherwise \emph{Oversize} \nis{80} when the
    longest side exceeds 100\,cm or the second exceeds 80\,cm (most of her canvases); otherwise
    \emph{Non-Conveyable} \nis{80} for 25--70\,kg actual weight, a wooden crate or a round tube.
  \item \textbf{Peak season (demand) surcharge} \cite{dhldemand}: from 1~October 2026 to
    5~February 2027, per kilogram of billing weight, by DHL's destination region: Americas
    (including the US) \nis{1.70}, Oceania \nis{1.70}, Europe \nis{0.90}, the rest of the world
    \nis{2.80}; Asia, China and the Middle East nothing.
  \item \textbf{Elevated Risk} \nis{119} per shipment to the destinations DHL lists (for example
    Ukraine, Venezuela, Haiti). DHL suspended the surcharge on Israel's own shipments on
    25~September 2026 \cite{dhlnotice}, so ordinary exports do not pay it.
  \item \textbf{Restricted Destination} \nis{119} per shipment to the destinations under UN
    sanctions that DHL lists.
  \item \textbf{Fuel surcharge}: a percentage that DHL sets every week from the US Gulf Coast jet
    fuel price, between 23\,\% and 56.25\,\% \cite{dhlsur}. It applies to transport and to the
    piece, peak season and elevated risk surcharges, but not to the restricted destination
    surcharge, insurance or customs services. For 12--18 October 2026 it is \textbf{49\,\%}
    (48\,\% the week before). The painter copies it into the settings.
  \item \textbf{Export declaration}: DHL's customs service, \nis{110} plus 18\,\% VAT
    $=$ \nis{129.80} per shipment \cite{dhlguide,dhlcustoms}, added when the works are worth more
    than the export declaration threshold (USD~200). If the painter files the declaration herself
    online, she unticks the setting and the fee goes.
  \item \textbf{Not estimated}: DHL's \emph{remote area} surcharge ($\max(\nis{95},\ \nis{2}$ per
    kg), plus fuel) depends on the exact address, and DHL publishes it only as a per-address
    lookup. Optional services (Saturday delivery, residential delivery and others) are not
    booked. When a remote-area charge happens, the seller absorbs it (about \nis{142} with fuel).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The formula}
With $W$ the shipment's billing weight, $z$ its zone, $T_z(W)$ the published transport price,
$d$ the account discount, $f$ the fuel surcharge, $P$ the piece surcharges, $D$ the peak season
surcharge ($\text{rate} \times W$), $E$ elevated risk, $R$ restricted destination and $X$ the
export declaration:
\[
  T' = T_z(W)\,(1-d), \qquad
  \text{delivery} \;=\; \Bigl\lceil\, T' + P + D + E + f\,\bigl(T' + P + D + E\bigr) + R + X
  \,\Bigr\rceil_{\nis{1}}
\]
Each product is rounded to the agora, and the total is rounded \emph{up} to a whole shekel. A
dollar order converts the shekel total once at the site's rate (\nis{3.7}) and rounds up to a
whole dollar.

\subsection{Insurance}
DHL's Shipment Insurance costs $\max(\nis{45},\ 1\,\%$ of the insured value$)$ \cite{dhlguide}.
The insured value is the lowest of the declared value, the work's own cap (when set) and the
\nis{10{,}000} cap in the settings. Insurance is included only abroad, and only when the shipment
goes through the DHL account; with manual shipping it is not, and the site does not say
``insured''. Example: a \nis{5{,}500} work is insured for \nis{55} (\usd{15}).

\subsection{When checkout asks for a quote, and the fallback table}\label{sec:quote}
Checkout asks the buyer to request a quote instead of showing a price for:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a crate, glazing, a tube for a work that cannot be rolled, a work marked ``quote only'',
    more than 70\,kg actual weight, or a piece beyond DHL's limits (300\,cm, 1{,}000\,kg);
  \item a declared value above \usd{2{,}500} (the site's cap for DHL);
  \item Europe, while its zone is off (EU sales wait for the GPSR requirements), and any country
    on the quote-only list.
\end{itemize}
Denied destinations (Iran, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq) and parcels to Great Britain worth up to
\pounds135 (where the seller would have to collect UK VAT) are refused. When the estimate is
switched off, or DHL's guide has no price for the destination, the painter's table prices the
parcel (zone $\times$ size class, plus its own fuel and season surcharges), as in Israel.

\subsection{Keeping the estimate current}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Every week or two}: copy DHL's international fuel surcharge for the coming week
    and its date into Settings $\to$ Shipping \cite{dhlsur}. After 14 days the settings page
    shows ``not updated''.
  \item \textbf{Every January}: DHL publishes a new rate guide. The new prices are added as a new
    tariff in \code{src/server/shipping/tariff/} (the old one stays for old dates), with the tests
    and this document; from 1 January 2027 the settings page reminds her.
  \item \textbf{When DHL announces it}: a new peak season window, changes to the elevated risk or
    restricted lists.
  \item \textbf{Once DHL opens an account}: the account discount, and the decision whether DHL
    files the export declaration.
  \item \textbf{When Israel Post publishes new price lists} (domestic and international): the
    numbers in \code{tariff/israel-post-2026.ts}, with the tests and this document.
  \item \textbf{Once a rolled parcel has been weighed}: the rolled weight assumption
    (\code{ROLLED\_BASE\_G}, \code{ROLLED\_G\_PER\_M2} in \code{rates.ts}), with the tests and
    this document.
\end{itemize}

\section{Israel Post}\label{sec:post}
Israel Post is offered next to the courier, in Israel and abroad, at the prices of its own price
lists \cite{postdomestic,postems}, whenever the parcel fits its limits. The code is
\code{src/server/shipping/israel-post.ts}; the data is in
\code{src/server/shipping/tariff/israel-post-2026.ts}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Each work is its own parcel}, priced by its \emph{actual} weight. Israel Post
    does not charge by volume, so for large, light canvases it costs a fraction of DHL's price.
  \item \textbf{Limits} \cite{postguide}: 20\,kg; no side over 150\,cm; the longest side
    plus twice the other two at most 300\,cm. Abroad, the destination's own limits apply
    \cite{postems}: 150 and 300\,cm for most countries, but 105 and 200\,cm for some, such as
    the United States, Germany, Italy and Australia (where the price list's entry is unclear, the
    site uses 105 and 200). Crates and glazed works are not sent by post.
  \item \textbf{EMS abroad}: the destination's group (1--13) sets the price per item: up to
    0.5\,kg, up to 1\,kg, then each further 0.5\,kg or part. EMS reaches 171 countries; where
    Israel Post sends no EMS (for example Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar) only DHL is offered, and
    Europe stays ``by quote'' while its zone is off. The site uses the counter prices; preparing
    the parcel online (``prepare at home'') is \nis{5} cheaper.
  \item \textbf{Insurance abroad}: Israel Post's liability extension, \nis{6.90} per
    \nis{1{,}000} of value or part of it, up to \nis{25{,}000} (a setting).
  \item \textbf{Handling}: the painter takes the parcel to the post office (with the customs
    form for EMS) and enters the tracking number on the order.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stretched or rolled}\label{sec:rolled}
At checkout the buyer chooses how a canvas travels: \emph{stretched}, on its wooden frame in its
box (the default), or \emph{rolled} in a tube, taken off the stretcher. A rolled canvas has to be
re-stretched on arrival, at the buyer's cost; the pre-contract disclosure and the order say so.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Which works}: the painter's canvases, unless she turns it off for a work (thick
    paint that could crack, for example). Framed, glazed or crated works always travel as listed.
  \item \textbf{The tube}: the shorter side of the painting plus 10\,cm long, $26\times26$\,cm
    across. Its weight is assumed at 1.5\,kg for the tube and wrapping plus 1.2\,kg per square
    metre of canvas, against the box's 1.5\,kg plus 6\,kg per square metre; the painter should
    weigh one rolled parcel to confirm it.
  \item \textbf{The effect}: a tube up to 150\,cm long fits Israel Post's 300\,cm limit, so
    large paintings can go by Israel Post at home and by EMS abroad; DHL bills the tube's smaller
    volume. The artwork page's ``Delivery from'' shows the cheaper of the two, marked ``rolled in
    a tube'' when that is the one.
\end{itemize}
For a $100\times100$\,cm canvas (box $112\times112\times11$\,cm, 7.5\,kg; tube
$110\times26\times26$\,cm, 2.7\,kg), shipping only, on 15 October 2026, in shekels:

\section{The quote lock}
When the buyer presses ``Continue to payment'', the server prices the order again from the same
rules. If anything differs from the page (for example, the fuel surcharge was updated in between)
the buyer sees ``the price changed'' and reloads. Otherwise the order stores the whole quote: the
method, the carrier, whether the table or the DHL estimate priced it, and the estimate's workings
(tariff, zone, weights, every surcharge, the fuel percentage and its date, the rounding). It also
stores the quote's version and, for dollar orders, the exchange rate. The works are held for
35~minutes. A payment that arrives for an outdated version is refunded automatically. Later
changes to the settings never change an existing order.

\section{Customs}
\paragraph{Leaving Israel.} Every international shipment carries a commercial invoice: HS code
9701.91 (export code 9701910000), ``Original painting, \ldots, hand-painted unique work of art'',
Incoterm DAP, reason ``Permanent export -- sale of an original work of art'', the line values and
a statement of Israeli origin (invoices to the EU also say the work is not concerned by
Regulation (EU) 2019/880). Above USD~200 an export declaration is required before the label is
bought; DHL files it for \nis{129.80} (included in the estimate), or the painter files it online.

\paragraph{Arriving.} Shipments are DAP: the buyer pays the destination's duties, import VAT and
any clearance fee to DHL on delivery, and ticks a box saying so before paying. The site shows a
notice; it never charges these amounts. Examples:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{United States}: original paintings (HTS 9701.91.00) are free of duty
    \cite{usitc}; DHL may charge a clearance fee, and above \usd{2{,}500} a formal entry is needed.
  \item \textbf{United Kingdom}: import VAT on works of art is charged on a quarter of the value,
    an effective 5\,\% \cite{hmrc}.
  \item \textbf{European Union}: import VAT at the member state's rate, which may be a reduced
    rate for works of art (Directive 2006/112/EC, art.~103 \cite{vatdir}; Germany 7\,\%
    \cite{ustg}). The temporary EUR~3 duty applies only to parcels up to EUR~150.
\end{itemize}
Prepaid duties (DDP; DHL's ``Duty Tax Paid'' costs 2\,\% of the duties, at least \nis{67}) are
not offered.

\section{VAT and tax documents}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The VAT rate is 18\,\% (since 1 January 2025).
  \item \textbf{Osek patur} (the current setting): no VAT is charged and receipts are issued. The
    2026 turnover ceiling is \nis{122{,}833} \cite{patur}; the site counts the works' prices
    (not delivery) and warns at 80\,\% and 95\,\%.
  \item \textbf{Osek murshe}: prices include VAT. In Israel the VAT is included in the whole total
    (works, delivery, insurance), and tax invoice-receipts are issued. A sale abroad is an export
    at 0\,\% VAT (VAT Law, section 30(a)(1) \cite{vatlaw}), supported by the export documents.
  \item DHL's international transport carries no Israeli VAT; its export declaration service does
    (hence \nis{129.80}). An \emph{osek murshe} can reclaim that VAT; an \emph{osek patur}
    cannot.
  \item Income tax is outside the site; it is the accountant's.
\end{itemize}

\section{Fees and commissions}
The seller pays every processing fee out of the price; the buyer's total never includes one.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cardcom} (cards, Bit, Apple Pay, Google Pay): the rates are set by the merchant
    agreement and are not public. The questions to ask (Israeli and foreign cards, the dollar
    terminal's conversion rate, who pays installment interest) are in the onboarding guide. Bit
    is offered for shekel totals up to \nis{5{,}000}.
  \item \textbf{PayPal} (buyers abroad): the published Israeli merchant fees \cite{paypal} are
    3.40\,\% plus a fixed fee (\usd{0.30} or \nis{1.20}), plus 1.00\,\% when the buyer's account is
    in the US, Canada or PayPal's ``Europe~I'' and ``Northern Europe'' groups (2.00\,\% elsewhere),
    plus 18\,\% VAT on the fees.
    Converting a dollar balance to shekels costs 2.5\,\% on the exchange rate (2.95\,\% with VAT).
    Example: a US buyer pays $\usd{1{,}490} + \usd{800} = \usd{2{,}290}$; the fee is
    $4.40\,\% \times 2{,}290 + 0.30 = \usd{101.06}$, \usd{119.25} with VAT (5.2\,\%), and
    converting the rest to shekels costs about \usd{64} more, so the seller keeps about 92\,\%.
  \item \textbf{Installments} are off (one payment); up to 36 can be enabled for Israeli card
    payments in shekels. Who pays the interest is still to be agreed with Cardcom.
  \item \textbf{DHL}: every DHL charge the seller pays is inside the delivery price above;
    the destination's clearance fees are the buyer's.
  \item \textbf{Cancellation}: when an Israeli buyer cancels for a change of mind, the seller may
    keep $\min(5\,\%,\ \nis{100})$ of the refund, once per order (Consumer Protection Law). It is
    the only fee the seller may withhold.
  \item \textbf{Gallery commission}: none on the website. A gallery that sells a work takes its
    commission out of the list price; it does not add to it (the pricing procedure, rule~2).
\end{itemize}

\section{Currency}
Shekels are the base; Israeli destinations always pay in shekels. Every work also has a stored
dollar price, $\operatorname{round}_{10}(\text{shekel price} / 3.7)$, and buyers abroad may pay in
dollars when every work in the order has one. Delivery and insurance are computed in shekels and
converted once at the site's rate (\nis{3.7} per dollar, set on 1 October 2026), each rounded up
to a whole dollar; the rate is stored with the order, and dollar payments are refunded in dollars.
The rate is a setting (Settings $\to$ Checkout) to update when the shekel moves.

\section{Open questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The export declaration: the threshold (the site uses USD~200) and who files it, DHL or
    the painter (DHL, the customs broker).
  \item Whether an \emph{osek patur} may export, and how the export is reported (the accountant).
  \item Cardcom's rates for Israeli and foreign cards, the dollar terminal, and installment
    interest (Cardcom).
  \item DHL's account discount, and whether residential or remote-area surcharges apply to her
    usual destinations (DHL).
\end{enumerate}
